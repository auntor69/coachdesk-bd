COACHDESK BD
============================================================================
The operating system for coaching centers in Bangladesh.

One app runs the whole center: admissions and roster with bulk import,
batches, attendance, exams and marks, a fee board with invoices,
installments, scholarships and a four-stage chase list, payroll,
expenses, notices, weekly progress reports, multi-branch HQ, and a
full audit log — in Bengali and English, offline-first, on any phone
the staff already owns.

----------------------------------------------------------------------------
Stack: Next.js (App Router) + Supabase (Postgres, RLS, Auth) + Tailwind,
deployed on Vercel. 98 SQL migrations. 1,811 unit tests and 1,559
row-level-security assertions, all green.
----------------------------------------------------------------------------

Three decisions that make it different:

1. Money is integers. Every amount is stored in poisha (1 taka =
   100 poisha). No float ever touches a balance — not in the app,
   not in the database.

2. Billing is burned, not billed. A center holds one prepaid balance
   and a database function charges each day against it — top-up,
   catch-up, refusal, every attempt audited. The books reconcile
   themselves. When the balance cannot cover the day, the app goes
   read-only: access is derived from money, not a subscription date.

3. Security is tested, not assumed. Plan ceilings and roles are
   enforced twice — SQL triggers first, server actions second — and
   every RLS change is proven against every table, every run.

Built for Bangladesh, not translated into it: Bengali-first UI, fees
in taka, bKash payments, month close the way centers actually run it.

Status: in active development — not launched yet.

Working today: the full center workflow above, held up by 1,811
unit tests and 1,559 security assertions, all green.

Terms exist now. Seven pages — the prepaid billing terms leading
it, then refunds, the terms of service, privacy, acceptable use,
account deletion and security — written in Bengali and English,
readable at /legal without an account, because the signup box and
the recharge screen both link into them. Agreeing is recorded as a
row: which wording of which document, by whom, and when. No page
claims a certification nobody has audited.

Shipped since: the prepaid billing engine. The balance, the daily
burn, self-serve recharge and the founder's review queue are live, and
the founder has two levers — money and granted days, each with a
written reason. The billing screen is built around the balance: how
long the money lasts, what is left, what it is priced from, how to add
more, and every day charged so far with its own count and rate. A
nightly run settles the centers nobody opened that day, so the live
balance is current before anyone looks. The old seat-based pricing was
retired on 2026-09-25: the buy controls are gone, the plan page is a
redirect, and a per-student tier no longer has a student ceiling at
all — size sets the daily rate instead of a door. The column that held
bought places is archived rather than dropped, so the payments that
bought them still explain themselves. The words went with the controls
on 2026-09-29: both dictionaries lost the buy-and-ask vocabulary whose
screens no longer exist, and the head office's card reads the asks
that were already filed as the history they now are.

The rest of the month went to the ways in. An account signs in with
exactly one of three things — a real email address, a mobile number,
or a username a center minted for a teacher who has neither — and the
screens now say which of the three an account holds, and set its
password, instead of assuming a number and drawing a blank card. The
founder can reach an owner who signed up with an email, reset their
password, and read the audit log without naming anybody by a number
they do not have. The login, signup and onboarding screens read the
reader's language and carry the switch to change it, and the app draws
its three meaningful colours from one place — a green for present, a
red for wrong, an amber for late — so no screen invents a second
shade of its own.

A building past the three an institution includes is bought, not
granted: it opens only when there is money on the balance to carry the
৳750 a month it adds, which is enforced by a trigger on `branches`
as well as by the form. A three-day signup is given days and not
money, so it runs the three buildings its tier includes and no more —
before that it could name fifty for nothing. Every other dimension a
plan prices is fenced the same way: the two teacher seats a centre
includes are the tier's and each one past them is ৳100 a month off the
balance, refused by a second trigger on `teachers` and by the action
behind the form, and the screens say why instead of offering a button
that could only be refused.

The last dimension a free account could grow is its roster, and it is
bounded by a number rather than by money — because a per-student
tier's price IS the student, a money rule there would refuse a trial's
very first admission. An organisation that has never funded its
balance holds up to five hundred active students, which is one bulk
import, and any money at all lifts the bound entirely. The number is
the founder's, set beside the three trial lengths on the same screen,
and zero switches the fence off. What is left unfenced is deliberate
and written down: the free SMS credits a signup is given, which the
OTP gate closes rather than the plan.

Still ahead: automated SMS gateway and the real bKash API
integration.
